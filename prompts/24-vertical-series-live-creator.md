# Vertical Series and Live-Creator Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for responsible live-commerce demonstrations, short episodic drama, recurring creator formats, and direct audience answers. Prioritize truthful claims, clear hand-object contact, readable hooks, and continuity that can survive a series.

## VER-001 Honest Modular Lunch-Jar Live Demo

![Original unbranded modular lunch-jar kit with five components](../assets/gallery/modular-lunch-jar-kit.webp)

**Use it for:** A compact live-commerce demonstration that shows verified assembly and fit without inventing performance, health, or certification claims.

**Mode:** Product image + consenting presenter + approved feature sheet + authorized voice  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact five-component lunch-jar kit; Image 2 = adult presenter identity and wardrobe; Text 1 = approved feature wording; Audio 1 = presenter voice identity.

```text
Create a vertical live-demo clip for the exact unbranded kit in Image 1: one sage-green jar, one brushed-steel inner cup, one cream screw lid, one dark-green folding spoon, and one woven carrying loop. Preserve all five components, threads, nesting geometry, colors, finishes, and presenter identity. Use only claims explicitly present in Text 1.

0–4 seconds: overhead view. The presenter points to each separated component once while saying with Audio 1, “Five parts, shown separately.” No generated captions. 4–9 seconds: medium close-up as the presenter places the inner cup into the jar, folds the spoon, and sets it inside without forcing or clipping. 9–13 seconds: the presenter screws the lid on until hand-tight, attaches the carrying loop to its actual anchor, then lifts the closed jar five centimeters above the table and returns it. 13–15 seconds: stable hero view with presenter behind the table and blank upper space for approved pricing or captions added later.

Sound intent: clear authorized speech, metal nesting, thread contact, quiet kitchen room tone, no music. Do not add food, steam, leak tests, temperature claims, capacity, certifications, discounts, urgency, extra components, generated text, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Five-part count, assembly order, thread and loop mechanics, hands, exact spoken claim, safe lift, and blank text area.

## VER-002 The Wrong Parcel, the Right Neighbor

**Use it for:** A warm vertical micro-drama with a fast hook, clear prop logic, two consenting adults, and a non-exploitative reversal.

**Mode:** Two adult identities + apartment corridor + two parcel references + authorized dialogue  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–2 = neighbors and wardrobe; Image 3 = corridor and door positions; Images 4–5 = two plain parcels distinguished only by blue and orange geometric stickers; Audios 1–2 = each actor's voice.

```text
Create a 15-second vertical micro-drama in the exact corridor from Image 3. Preserve Neighbor A at the blue door on screen left, Neighbor B at the orange door on screen right, both adult identities, wardrobe, two parcel sizes, sticker colors, door numbers represented only by shapes, and the open center path. No addresses or private data.

0–4 seconds, hook: both doors open at once. Each neighbor looks down and picks up the parcel outside the wrong door—A takes the orange-sticker parcel, B takes the blue-sticker parcel. Insert close-up clearly shows the swapped colors. 4–9 seconds: they turn inward, notice each other's parcel, and freeze. A uses Audio 1: “I think our morning got crossed.” B listens, checks the sticker, then smiles. 9–13 seconds: they walk to center and exchange parcels hand to hand once; B uses Audio 2: “Good thing the hallway is short.” 13–15 seconds: each holds the correct parcel, nods, and returns toward the correct colored door.

Natural corridor ambience, two door sounds, no music or subtitles. Keep one blue parcel, one orange parcel, exact hand ownership, speaker turns, eyelines, and screen geography. Avoid romance cues, conflict, surveillance imagery, readable labels, extra neighbors, duplicate boxes, thrown parcels, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Parcel color logic, door geography, identity, voice ownership, exchange count, readable hook, privacy, and final state.

## VER-003 One Tool, Three Bicycle Fixes

**Use it for:** A repeatable creator-series opener that establishes an expert, one workbench, and three visually distinct maintenance topics without unsafe instruction.

**Mode:** Consenting creator + exact workshop + rights-cleared bicycle parts + approved procedure cards  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = adult bicycle mechanic identity and apron; Image 2 = locked workbench; Images 3–5 = loose brake lever, dry chain sample, and misaligned bell on non-ridden display parts; Texts 1–3 = technician-approved action boundaries.

```text
Create a vertical series opener titled only in post-production; generate no on-screen text. Preserve the adult mechanic, green apron, bench, blue repair mat, one hex key, one cloth, three display parts, camera height, and clean hand ownership. These are demonstration parts off the bicycle; no riding or safety guarantee is shown.

0–2 seconds: direct-to-camera medium shot. The mechanic places one hex key on the mat and says, “One tool, three checks.” 2–6 seconds: close-up of the loose display brake lever; the mechanic indicates the approved fastener from Text 1 but stops before tightening. 6–10 seconds: match cut to the dry chain sample; the mechanic wipes one link with the cloth according to Text 2, with no liquid added. 10–14 seconds: match cut to the display bell; the mechanic rotates it to the approved neutral position from Text 3 and rings it once. 14–15 seconds: return to the same medium shot; the mechanic points to three blank side areas reserved for episode labels added later.

Sound intent: exact dialogue, metal touch, cloth, one bell ring, quiet workshop, no music. Avoid implying a complete safety inspection, torque values, lubrication claims, road use, extra tools, spinning wheels, generated captions, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Creator continuity, tool count, part separation, approved boundaries, match cuts, dialogue, blank label zones, and no unsafe completeness claim.

## VER-004 Ceramic Creator Answers One Real Question

**Use it for:** A direct audience-answer format that turns one verified craft question into a concise visual explanation with transparent limits.

**Mode:** Consenting creator + approved question/script + clay demonstration references + workshop ambience  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = adult ceramicist identity and apron; Image 2 = exact worktable; Images 3–4 = fictional leather-hard mug body and handle before joining; Text 1 = approved audience question and answer; Audio 1 = creator voice.

```text
Create a vertical answer video in the ceramic studio. Preserve the adult ceramicist, indigo apron, mug body, handle, scoring tool, slip brush, worktable, clay moisture state, camera, and hands. Use the approved script in Text 1 word for word; the source question will be typeset later, so leave a blank upper-left area and generate no comment interface.

0–3 seconds: medium shot. The creator looks into camera and says with Audio 1, “Why can a mug handle separate at the join?” 3–8 seconds: macro demonstration on two disposable clay samples, not a finished usable mug. The creator shows one smooth dry contact surface, then the approved scored-and-slipped sample from Image 4. Voice continues: “A weak join can start with mismatched moisture or too little surface preparation.” 8–12 seconds: side view as the creator presses the prepared sample together once with even support; no claim that this guarantees success. 12–15 seconds: return to medium shot. The creator says, “Use your studio’s process, then test before firing.” Hold with blank caption space.

Sound intent: clear voice, light clay and tool sounds, Audio 1 room tone, no music. Avoid generated comments, universal guarantees, kiln instructions, sharp-tool misuse, real customer identity, extra hands, changing clay state, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Exact question/answer, creator identity, clay-state continuity, safe tool handling, demonstration limits, blank UI area, and no guarantee.
