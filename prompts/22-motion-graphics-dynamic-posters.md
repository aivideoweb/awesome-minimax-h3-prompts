# Motion Graphics and Dynamic Poster Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for poster assembly, product feature cards, exhibition openers, and modular motion identities. Keep typography out of generation when exact copy matters: reserve stable blank plates and composite approved text afterward.

## MOG-001 Night-Market Poster Builds on the Beat

![Original paper-collage night-market poster with a blank typography panel](../assets/gallery/dynamic-night-market-poster.webp)

**Use it for:** Turning a finished key visual into a controlled vertical poster reveal with exact element order and a clean typography handoff.

**Mode:** Last-frame image + optional original music reference  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact final composition and last frame; Audio 1 = original four-phrase rhythm only.

```text
Create a tactile paper-collage animation that ends exactly on Image 1. Preserve the midnight-blue field, one indigo arch, five amber lanterns, two teal ribbons, three stall awnings, foliage, coral accents, and one blank cream panel. Every element is a separate physical paper layer with small contact shadows; nothing may turn into readable text.

0–4 seconds: begin on the empty midnight-blue paper field. The indigo arch assembles from three pieces with short, dry paper-slide sounds. 4–8 seconds: three market stalls rise from the lower edge in left-to-right order; their awnings unfold once and remain fixed. 8–12 seconds: five lanterns appear one at a time across the arch on successive beats, then the two teal ribbons sweep in from opposite sides without crossing the blank panel. 12–15 seconds: foliage and coral accents settle; the cream panel lifts gently into the lower-middle and the camera holds until the exact Image 1 composition is reached.

Use Audio 1 only for rhythm; add restrained paper friction and soft wooden taps. Keep a locked camera, readable layer order, stable colors, exact counts, and final safe margins. Avoid generated letters, fake language, extra stalls or lanterns, particle confetti, neon glow, camera zoom, copied festival branding, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Final-frame match, object counts, reveal order, beat timing, blank-panel stability, paper depth, and absence of accidental characters.

## MOG-002 Modular Product Feature Cards

**Use it for:** Presenting a small product system as clean, reusable motion modules without fabricating specifications or on-screen copy.

**Mode:** Product views + component sheet + approved color tokens + optional beat track  
**Format:** 1:1, target 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = exact fictional desk organizer from front, side, and top; Image 4 = approved removable components; Image 5 = four color swatches only; Audio 1 = original beat timing.

```text
Create a square motion-graphics feature sequence for the exact unbranded desk organizer in Images 1–4. Preserve its rounded ash-wood tray, grey recycled-felt divider, brass pen rail, two removable cups, all dimensions, seams, slots, material colors, and component count. Use only the four background colors from Image 5.

0–3 seconds: the complete organizer rotates no more than 20 degrees on a flat cream background, then stops in the Image 1 front view. 3–6 seconds: hard cut to a teal card; the two cups lift straight up from their real slots and pause beside the tray, with thin neutral leader lines ending at blank circular callout nodes. 6–9 seconds: cut to a muted coral card; the felt divider slides along its actual groove once, showing the two approved positions from Image 4. 9–12 seconds: cut to a charcoal card; every component returns to the correct assembled state, then the full product eases to center with an empty lower strip for verified labels added later.

Sound intent: Audio 1, wood contact, felt slide, no voiceover. Do not invent dimensions, capacities, certifications, magnets, hidden compartments, extra components, generated words, logos, floating geometry, impossible assembly, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Cross-view geometry, part count, slot causality, material fidelity, color-token use, blank callout nodes, and final assembly.

## MOG-003 Museum Object Silhouette Opener

**Use it for:** A short exhibition opener that derives motion from rights-cleared object shapes while keeping interpretation and provenance outside the generated video.

**Mode:** Four object silhouettes + approved palette + original percussion  
**Format:** 16:9, target 10 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = silhouette and relative scale of four rights-cleared fictional teaching replicas; Image 5 = approved palette; Audio 1 = four original percussion events.

```text
Create a restrained exhibition opener using only flat silhouettes of the four teaching replicas: a wide ceramic bowl, a narrow weaving shuttle, a small brass compass, and a folded paper fan. Preserve each outline and the relative scale shown in Images 1–4. Do not reproduce surface decoration, labels, dates, cultures, makers, or provenance claims.

0–2 seconds: the bowl silhouette enters from below and becomes a shallow horizon curve. 2–4 seconds: the shuttle travels left to right along that curve, leaving one thin line that stops at frame center. 4–6 seconds: the compass silhouette rotates exactly one quarter turn above the line and reveals a circular negative-space window. 6–8 seconds: the fan opens inside that window with six clearly separated folds. 8–10 seconds: all four silhouettes settle into a balanced horizontal composition, leaving a clean upper-right area for an approved exhibition title added in post.

Map one action to each Audio 1 percussion event. Use only Image 5 colors, crisp edges, subtle paper grain, and a locked camera. Avoid generated typography, historical reconstruction, decorative invention, cultural motifs not present in the inputs, extra objects, glossy 3D rendering, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Silhouette fidelity, relative scale, action-to-sound mapping, interpretive restraint, title safe area, and no invented cultural detail.

## MOG-004 Material-Swatch Motion Identity

**Use it for:** Creating a reusable, logo-safe motion bumper from an original set of materials rather than imitating an existing brand package.

**Mode:** Four original material swatches + approved abstract end mark + original sonic sting  
**Format:** 16:9 and center-safe for 1:1 crop, target 8 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = paper, cork, brushed aluminum, and woven fabric texture only; Image 5 = exact original abstract end mark; Audio 1 = authorized two-note sonic sting.

```text
Create an eight-second abstract motion identity using only the supplied material swatches and end mark. Start on a neutral warm-grey field. Preserve the real scale and directional grain of each material; never turn a texture into a product, landscape, letter, or recognizable logo.

0–2 seconds: one cream paper strip folds into a clean diagonal plane. 2–4 seconds: a cork tile slides under it while a narrow brushed-aluminum bar travels across the fold and stops at center. 4–6 seconds: woven fabric pulls through the resulting gap and forms a soft circular loop. 6–8 seconds: the four materials align through simple match cuts into the exact abstract geometry of Image 5, then hold through the final note of Audio 1.

Use real contact shadows, controlled easing, and centered crop safety. Sound intent: paper fold, cork tap, metal slide, textile pull, plus Audio 1. Avoid letters, slogans, invented logos, copied motion-brand conventions, liquid morphs, excessive reflections, extra colors, particles, or watermark.
```

**Review:** End-mark match, material direction, crop safety, clean easing, sound synchronization, and originality of the motion system.
