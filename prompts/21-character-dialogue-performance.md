# Character, Dialogue and Performance Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for character-led dialogue, restrained emotion, bilingual exchange, and ensemble performance. These recipes prioritize speaker ownership, micro-actions, eye lines, reaction timing, voice consent, and readable emotional change.

## CHR-001 Paper Birds Plan for the Storm

![Original indigo and saffron paper birds inside a storm-lit greenhouse](../assets/gallery/paper-birds-storm-shelter.webp)

**Use it for:** A compact two-character scene with exact voices, clear staging, tactile animation, and a complete emotional turn.

**Mode:** Character/environment image + two authorized voice references + storm ambience  
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact birds, greenhouse, seed tray, lantern, and exit; Audio 1 = indigo bird voice; Audio 2 = saffron bird voice; Audio 3 = rain and greenhouse creaks only.

```text
Create a tactile stop-motion paper-craft scene beginning from Image 1. Preserve exactly two characters: the broad indigo bird stays screen left with one folded wing held close; the smaller saffron bird stays screen right with a long pointed tail. Preserve paper colors, fold patterns, fiber texture, scale difference, seed tray, warm lantern, greenhouse structure, rain direction, and open door at far right.

0–3 seconds: establishing medium-wide shot with Audio 3 prominent. A gust rattles the greenhouse; both birds look toward the right-hand door, then at each other. No dialogue. 3–7 seconds: close on the indigo bird using Audio 1, low and steady: “The roof will hold if we brace the west hinge.” Its beak movement is minimal and synchronized; the saffron bird listens without speaking. 7–11 seconds: reverse close-up using Audio 2, quick but clear: “Then I’ll tie the twine. You keep the lantern dry.” The indigo bird listens and lowers its tense shoulder fold. 11–15 seconds: return to the two-shot. They each take one end of a loose natural-fiber twine and move together toward the exit; the lantern remains inside. End before they enter the rain.

Use slight stop-motion cadence, consistent eye lines, and one speaker at a time. Sound hierarchy: dialogue first, rain and wooden creaks below, no music. Avoid extra birds, human hands, realistic feathers, changing folds, swapped voices, subtitles, speech bubbles, a repaired roof not yet shown, exaggerated panic, franchise resemblance, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Two-character lock, speaker/voice ownership, exact dialogue, listening reactions, left/right staging, paper folds, twine continuity, and ending state.

## CHR-002 The First New Root

**Use it for:** A quiet live-action performance driven by a small physical discovery rather than melodramatic expression.

**Mode:** Consenting adult identity + greenhouse location + exact plant cutting reference  
**Format:** 4:5, target 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = adult horticulturist identity and wardrobe; Image 2 = small night greenhouse; Images 3–4 = exact fictional plant cutting and propagation jar before/after state.

```text
Create a restrained 12-second performance in the small greenhouse at blue hour. Preserve the adult horticulturist's identity, short silver hair, moss-green work shirt, round clear glasses, hands, and natural skin texture from Image 1. Preserve the narrow bench, task lamp, rain-speckled glass, single labeled-by-color but text-free propagation jar, leaf count, stem shape, water level, and root location from the references.

0–4 seconds: locked medium shot. The horticulturist enters frame carrying the jar with both hands, sits, and places it within the task lamp. Their posture is tired; breathing is shallow but calm. 4–8 seconds: macro insert of the glass. They rotate the jar less than a quarter turn, revealing the one new white root from Image 4. One fingertip stops just short of touching the glass. 8–12 seconds: return to a gentle close-up. Their eyes refocus, shoulders release, and a very small smile appears as they exhale once. They look back at the root, not at camera. Hold the final second.

Sound intent: rain on glass, stool creak, jar contact, one soft exhale, no dialogue or music. Keep the emotional progression fatigue → uncertainty → recognition → quiet relief. Avoid tears, celebration, time-lapse growth, extra roots, changing leaf count, scientific or success claims, readable label text, beauty retouching, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Identity, plant topology, root reveal timing, hand anatomy, gaze target, subtle emotional arc, and absence of exaggerated acting.

## CHR-003 Bilingual Radio-Repair Handoff

**Use it for:** A respectful bilingual exchange where exact speaker turns, prop continuity, pronunciation, and listening behavior matter.

**Mode:** Two consenting adult references + repair-shop image + authorized voices + approved dialogue  
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = technician identity and apron; Image 2 = customer identity and coat; Image 3 = exact repair counter and fictional radio; Audio 1 = technician voice; Audio 2 = customer voice.

```text
Create a natural service handoff in a small independent radio-repair shop. Keep the technician screen left behind the counter, customer screen right, radio centered between them, and open walking path visible. Preserve both adult identities, wardrobe, glasses, radio geometry, tuning dial, handle, counter scratches, tool locations, eyelines, and respectful distance. No readable customer records or real brand marks.

0–4 seconds, medium two-shot: the technician uses Audio 1 and says in Japanese, “修理が終わりました。音を確認しますか。” The customer listens with no mouth movement, then nods once. 4–9 seconds, radio insert and customer reaction: the technician turns the existing dial one click; a quiet fictional jazz phrase plays. The customer uses Audio 2 and replies in English, “Yes, that sounds like home again.” The technician listens and keeps both hands away from the dial. 9–15 seconds: back to two-shot. The technician switches the radio off, folds the existing cloth around it, and says in Japanese, “大切にお使いください。” The customer bows slightly; end before payment or personal details.

Keep exact language, speaker ownership, lip sync, pauses, action causality, and audio perspective. Dialogue is foreground; shop room tone and radio remain lower. Avoid subtitles baked into the image, voice swapping, accents used as comedy, additional customers, invented warranty claims, duplicated tools, brand labels, private data, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Japanese accuracy, English delivery, voice consent, turn-taking, listening faces, radio state, hand ownership, privacy, and no policy claims.

## CHR-004 One Extra Meal: Three-Person Ensemble

**Use it for:** A short ensemble scene where each adult has a distinct objective and the story resolves through reactions and blocking.

**Mode:** Three consenting adult references + community-kitchen location + prop sheet + optional room tone  
**Format:** 2.39:1, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = coordinator, bicycle courier, and late-arriving worker identities/wardrobe; Image 4 = exact kitchen pass and exits; Image 5 = two sealed meal boxes and one insulated bag; Audio 1 = kitchen closing ambience.

```text
Create an understated ensemble scene during closing time at a fictional community kitchen. Preserve the three adult identities and roles: coordinator in a rust apron behind the pass, bicycle courier in a reflective rain shell beside the left exit, late-arriving worker in a grey coat entering from the right. Preserve the kitchen geography, two sealed meal boxes, one insulated delivery bag, wet floor mat, clock without readable time, and all screen directions.

0–4 seconds: wide master. The coordinator counts two meal boxes, places one into the courier's bag, then notices the other has no pickup marker. The courier waits and looks toward the coordinator, not camera. 4–8 seconds: medium on the right doorway as the late worker arrives, slightly out of breath, checks the empty pickup shelf, and tries to hide disappointment. No dialogue. 8–12 seconds: return to master. The coordinator and courier exchange a brief look; the courier removes the first box from the bag and gestures toward the late worker. The coordinator instead leaves that box with the courier and hands the untouched second sealed box to the late worker. 12–15 seconds: the late worker accepts it with both hands and quietly says, “Thank you.” The coordinator nods; the courier closes the bag containing the original first box. Hold all three in frame.

Sound intent: Audio 1, rain at exits, bag zipper, one line of clean dialogue, no score. Keep exactly two boxes throughout; no box is opened, duplicated, or transferred twice. Avoid charity spectacle, infantilizing reactions, logos, readable personal data, unsafe food handling, crowd extras, tears, speeches, continuity jumps, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Three identities, geography, box count, bag state, eyelines, reaction order, dialogue ownership, respectful tone, and complete resolution.
