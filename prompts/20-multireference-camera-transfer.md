# Multi-Reference and Camera-Transfer Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for long takes, camera-grammar transfer, multi-image match actions, and reference-led tutorials. Each input receives one narrow role so that style, identity, motion, product, and factual instructions do not compete.

## MRF-001 Three-Biome Museum Rail in One Take

**Status:** Concept — not independently tested

![Original miniature museum rail spanning three tabletop biomes](../assets/gallery/three-biome-museum-rail.webp)

**Use it for:** A continuous macro journey through a handcrafted exhibition while keeping scale, route, and material transitions legible.

**Mode:** First-frame image + biome detail references + optional mechanical sound reference  
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds, one continuous take  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact first frame and table geometry; Images 2–4 = red-clay canyon, clear-water wetland, and moss forest material details only; Audio 1 = cart wheel and rail texture, not music.

```text
Create one uninterrupted macro tracking shot beginning exactly from Image 1. The same tiny unbranded brass inspection cart travels forward on the one continuous rail from the red-clay canyon, across the clear-water wetland, and into the mossy cloud forest. Preserve the walnut table, archival cabinets, museum task lights, cart dimensions, wheel count, rail gauge, biome order, and handcrafted miniature scale.

0–4 seconds: low rear three-quarter follow as the cart starts in the canyon. Fine red dust shifts under the rail supports; the cart never leaves the track. 4–9 seconds: camera arcs gently to the side while the rail crosses the wetland on visible brass supports. Water reacts with small ripples but never floods the cart. 9–14 seconds: camera lowers behind one moss ridge, allowing the ridge to create a natural foreground wipe, then reveals the cart entering the cloud forest. Condensation becomes slightly denser and the task light softens through it. 14–15 seconds: the cart stops beneath one miniature tree and the camera holds.

No cuts, teleports, speed ramps, hidden resets, or changes of scale. Sound intent: Audio 1 wheel texture, faint water movement, distant museum ventilation, no narration or music. Avoid real species claims, floating rail, duplicated cart, changing wheel count, full-size landscape cues, people, labels, generated text, logos, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Genuine continuous take, one cart, uninterrupted rail, biome order, material boundaries, water interaction, miniature scale, and clean ending.

## MRF-002 Radial Cork Speaker: Transfer Motion Grammar, Not Content

**Status:** Concept — not independently tested

![Original fictional graphite-and-cork portable speaker](../assets/gallery/radial-cork-speaker.webp)

**Use it for:** Reusing the pacing and camera grammar of a rights-cleared reference clip while building a wholly new product film.

**Mode:** Product image + rights-cleared camera reference video + original beat track + material detail images  
**Format:** 16:9, target 12 seconds  
**Reference map:** Image 1 = exact speaker geometry and materials; Video 1 = camera path, shot duration, and transition timing only; Audio 1 = original beat grid; Images 2–3 = cork and woven-grille texture only.

```text
Create an original studio advertisement for the fictional graphite-and-cork portable speaker in Image 1. Transfer only the abstract camera grammar from Video 1: shot lengths, direction of travel, acceleration curve, focal transitions, and cut timing. Do not copy its subject, setting, colors, props, lighting design, composition, campaign idea, text, or recognizable effects.

0–3 seconds: slow three-quarter push toward the full speaker on a charcoal plinth; amber rim light reveals the cork edge. 3–6 seconds: cut on Audio 1's second downbeat to a controlled lateral macro move across the cork pores and metal seam. 6–9 seconds: repeat Video 1's orbit speed around the circular woven grille while the product remains stationary and physically intact. The amber status light turns on once at the final beat of this section. 9–12 seconds: pull back to the original three-quarter product view; a shallow pattern of light crosses the acoustic panel behind it and settles, leaving clean negative space for typography added later.

Preserve the exact silhouette, circular grille, cork panels, seam placement, amber indicator, scale, and material response from Image 1. Sound intent: Audio 1 plus restrained tactile clicks and low room tone; do not imply measured loudness or acoustic performance. Avoid logos, generated copy, extra controls, duplicate speakers, pulsing geometry, visible sound waves, floating product, copied reference-video scenery, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Product invariants, separation between motion grammar and source content, beat timing, material fidelity, status-light causality, and claim safety.

## MRF-003 Four Workshops, One Copper Thread

**Status:** Concept — not independently tested

**Use it for:** A multi-image craft film that connects independent makers through one continuous match action rather than a copied montage.

**Mode:** Four location/hand references + one material reference + optional original music  
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = exact fictional ceramic, textile, paper, and electronics workspaces; Image 5 = one copper thread spool geometry and color; Audio 1 = original four-beat phrase.

```text
Create a four-part craft film connected by one fictional copper-colored thread. Each workspace, tool set, adult maker, and hand belongs only to its reference image. Never blend identities or transfer tools between locations. The same thread color and thickness remain visually continuous across cuts, but every handoff is an editorial match action rather than a claim that the spaces are physically connected.

0–4 seconds, ceramic studio: a maker draws the thread across a soft clay tile to mark one curved guide, moving screen left to right. At the thread crossing the lens, cut on Audio 1 beat one. 4–8 seconds, textile bench: another maker pulls the thread from the same screen position and completes two visible stitches in undyed canvas. A fabric fold occludes the frame for beat two. 8–11 seconds, paper studio: a third maker uses the thread to bind a small blank notebook with three holes; the closing cover creates the next cut. 11–15 seconds, electronics bench: a fourth maker lays an insulated copper lead into an approved channel of a simple unpowered light sculpture, then removes both hands. The sculpture emits one gentle amber glow only after the verified switch is pressed.

Keep screen direction, thread thickness, hand ownership, tool count, and step order clear. Sound intent: distinct material sounds over Audio 1, no dialogue. Avoid logos, unsafe electrical work, implied shared location, extra fingers, thread changing into wire before the final scene, invented sustainability claims, readable text, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Reference ownership, match-action positions, screen direction, material change boundary, safe final interaction, and no false production claim.

## MRF-004 Field Water-Filter Setup from Verified References

**Status:** Concept — not independently tested

**Use it for:** Turning approved product and procedure references into a concise tutorial without inventing parts, steps, or purification claims.

**Mode:** Product images + verified step sheet + consenting demonstrator + location reference  
**Format:** 9:16, target 15 seconds  
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = exact fictional filter kit from four angles; Image 5 = consenting adult demonstrator and wardrobe; Image 6 = approved campsite bench; Text 1 = manufacturer-reviewed setup order and safety wording.

```text
Create a vertical setup tutorial for the exact unbranded gravity water-filter kit in Images 1–4. Use only the steps and claims approved in Text 1. Preserve the two bags, hose, clamp, filter body, cap, connector shapes, color coding, demonstrator identity, wardrobe, bench, and hand ownership.

0–3 seconds: overhead inventory view shows exactly the approved parts laid separately on the dry bench. 3–6 seconds: medium close-up as the demonstrator connects the marked inlet hose to the matching filter port; show the connector seating fully, with no liquid present. 6–9 seconds: the demonstrator hangs the source bag from the approved hook and keeps the clean bag below it without either bag touching the ground. 9–12 seconds: close view of the approved flushing step from Text 1, keeping the discharge away from the clean bag. 12–15 seconds: stable wide view of the completed arrangement, flow stopped, with blank lower safe area for verified captions added in post.

Camera cuts only to clarify contact points. Sound intent: fabric, connector click, light water, outdoor ambience, no voiceover. Do not show anyone drinking, state that water is safe, invent a flow rate, skip the flush, reverse inlet/outlet, add parts, bake generated instructions into frame, show a logo, or add watermark.
```

**Review:** Part count, connector mapping, approved sequence, bag height, contamination boundaries, demonstrator safety, and absence of unverified efficacy claims.
