# Education, Documentary and Science Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for explainers, museum media, field documentation, and procedural education. Verify all facts with qualified sources before publication.

## EDU-001 Tidal Marsh Carbon Explainer

**Use it for:** A short science explainer that keeps process order and visual evidence separate from narration.

**Mode:** Diagram image + rights-cleared field footage + narration reference  \
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Image 1 = verified process relationships; Video 1 = habitat texture and species behavior only; Audio 1 = authorized narrator voice.

```text
Create a concise educational sequence explaining how a fictionalized temperate salt marsh stores carbon. Follow the verified relationship in Image 1 without copying its graphic style or labels. Use Video 1 only for mud, cordgrass, shallow water, and natural wind movement; do not imply that the footage proves a specific numerical claim.

0–4 seconds: eye-level field view as the tide moves gently between grass stems. 4–9 seconds: camera passes below the waterline in a clean visual transition, revealing roots and suspended organic particles settling into layered sediment. 9–13 seconds: simplify the scene into an original cutaway model showing plant growth above and gradual burial below. 13–15 seconds: return to the real marsh view with empty lower-third space.

Narration intent: plain language, one idea per sentence, no unverified percentages or universal claims. Sound intent: soft wind, water movement, one restrained transition tone. Preserve correct flow direction, root continuity, scale cues, and species count. Avoid false time-lapse speed, invented data, labels generated inside the video, disaster imagery, copied infographics, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Scientific sequence, claim scope, field-footage context, scale, and accessible pacing.

## EDU-002 Museum Object Story Without Reconstruction Claims

**Use it for:** A museum-screen vignette that distinguishes documented object detail from interpretive atmosphere.

**Mode:** Object turntable images + conservation notes + optional ambient reference  \
**Format:** 16:9, target 12 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–4 = exact artifact geometry, damage, and surface; Text note = verified provenance facts; Audio 1 = room tone only.

```text
Produce a quiet museum display film for a fictional 19th-century brass navigation instrument. Preserve the object's shape, engraved marks, patina, scratches, and missing fastener exactly from Images 1–4. Do not restore damage or invent readable inscriptions.

Opening: controlled macro view of the worn adjustment wheel under neutral conservation light. Middle: camera travels slowly across the engraved arc while a narrow highlight reveals tool marks; transition into a restrained silhouette of a shipboard table, clearly presented as atmospheric interpretation rather than historical reconstruction. Ending: return to a full three-quarter object view on a neutral support, leaving clean space for curator-approved captions added in post.

Sound intent: quiet gallery air, soft wood creak, no dialogue or dramatic score. Keep the artifact static, scale consistent, and reflections physically plausible. Avoid hands operating the fragile object, fabricated dates, national symbols, readable generated labels, restored polish, sensational adventure imagery, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Object fidelity, damage preservation, evidence/interpretation boundary, caption space, and cultural context.

## EDU-003 Safe Workshop Procedure: Drill-Press Setup

**Use it for:** A visual training insert that demonstrates preparation steps without replacing qualified instruction.

**Mode:** Equipment images + approved procedure card + instructor reference  \
**Format:** 16:9, target 15 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = exact machine controls and guards; Text 1 = organization-approved sequence; Image 4 = consenting adult instructor identity and PPE.

```text
Create a workshop safety overview showing setup before a drill press is powered. This is supplementary visual communication, not standalone certification. Preserve the machine, guard, emergency stop, clamp, and instructor from the references.

Show exactly four steps in verified order: instructor disconnects power; checks that the correct bit is secured using the approved key and removes the key; clamps a plain wood sample flat to the table; adjusts the transparent guard and steps back with hands clear. Use medium and close views only where each action is visible. End before the machine starts, on a wide confirmation frame showing clear workspace, eye protection, secured material, and both hands away from controls.

Sound intent: natural room tone and four subtle confirmation clicks; no spoken claims. Keep control locations, hand placement, PPE, and tool geometry stable. Avoid operating the machine, loose clothing, gloves near rotating parts, unsafe demonstrations, invented compliance labels, readable generated instructions, brand marks, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Approved step order, hand safety, machine state, PPE, disclaimers, and local policy alignment.

## EDU-004 Microscopic World to Everyday Scale

**Use it for:** A visually engaging classroom transition that clearly separates illustrative scale from literal footage.

**Mode:** Verified micrograph + everyday-object image + narration script  \
**Format:** 9:16, target 10 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Image 1 = cell-wall topology and color-independent structure; Image 2 = exact leaf and tabletop composition; Text 1 = approved explanation.

```text
Create a vertical educational transition from an illustrative plant-cell interior to an everyday leaf. Begin inside a stylized but structurally informed cell: one visible nucleus, green chloroplast forms, a large central vacuole, and a rigid outer wall. A pulse of light moves through chloroplasts as the camera travels outward; cell walls become a mosaic, the mosaic becomes leaf tissue, and the camera exits through a dew droplet onto the exact leaf from Image 2.

The transition must make scale change legible through continuous zoom and stable landmarks. End on a child's gloved hand placing the leaf beside a blank observation card, with safe space for reviewed labels added later. Narration intent: identify this as an illustration and avoid implying that organelles have the shown colors at natural scale. Sound intent: soft tonal rise, room ambience, no music lyrics.

Avoid impossible organelle counts, literal-camera claims, generated labels, humanized cells, abrupt teleportation, anatomical confusion, copyrighted textbook style, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Conceptual accuracy, scale signaling, illustrative disclaimer, transition continuity, and age-appropriate clarity.
