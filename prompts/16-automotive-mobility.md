# Automotive and Mobility Video Prompts

Original MiniMax H3 prompts for vehicles, micromobility, transit, and delivery systems. Use closed or controlled environments for risky movement and never present generated footage as safety evidence.

## MOB-001 Electric City Car Interior Demo

**Use it for:** A premium interior feature film with exact control-to-state causality and no unsupported performance claims.

**Mode:** Vehicle interior images + UI state images + approved interaction map  \
**Format:** 16:9, target 12 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = cockpit geometry and materials; Images 4–6 = exact UI states; Text 1 = approved control behavior.

```text
Create a stationary interior demonstration for the fictional compact electric car “MORA C1” inside a controlled studio. Preserve the steering wheel, center display, physical buttons, seat stitching, vents, and materials from Images 1–3. The vehicle does not move.

Beat 1: macro view as an adult hand presses the physical climate button once; cut to the exact approved climate screen from Image 4. Beat 2: the same hand rotates the console dial one click; ambient lighting changes from cool white to muted amber according to Image 5. Beat 3: camera slides behind the front seats as the panoramic shade closes at realistic speed; finish on the approved calm-cabin state from Image 6.

Keep hand anatomy, reflections, control locations, UI text, screen brightness, and cause/effect order exact. Sound intent: button click, dial detent, shade motor, quiet cabin, no engine sound or voiceover. Avoid driving, invented range or safety claims, floating UI, screen gibberish, extra controls, logo mutation, fingerprints disappearing between cuts, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Cabin geometry, UI fidelity, input/state mapping, hand continuity, reflections, and claim safety.

## MOB-002 Cargo Bicycle Rain-Test Film

**Use it for:** A practical mobility product story showing weather handling without implying certification.

**Mode:** Bicycle reference images + adult rider image + closed-course location image  \
**Format:** 4:5, target 10–12 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = bicycle geometry and cargo cover; Image 4 = consenting rider identity and wardrobe; Image 5 = controlled route.

```text
Create a controlled rain-condition demonstration for a fictional front-loader cargo bicycle. Preserve frame geometry, wheel sizes, steering linkage, cargo box, reflective strips, and transparent rain cover from Images 1–3. Preserve the adult rider's identity, helmet, waterproof jacket, and footwear from Image 4.

Open on a stationary close view of water beading on the closed cargo cover. Cut to a low side tracking shot as the rider starts smoothly on the empty closed course from Image 5, both wheels maintaining ground contact. Show one broad low-speed turn, realistic tire spray, visible steering linkage, and stable covered cargo represented by two secured foam blocks. End with a controlled stop and one foot planted.

Sound intent: rainfall, tire hiss, chain, brake contact, no music. Do not claim waterproofing, braking distance, payload, or road legality. Avoid public traffic, child passengers, skidding, missing helmet, warped wheels, changing cargo, impossible steering, visible brands, captions, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Vehicle mechanics, rider anatomy, safety context, water behavior, cargo persistence, and no implied certification.

## MOB-003 Night Train Sleeper Service

**Use it for:** A hospitality-focused rail film that keeps cabin geography, staff identity, and service sequence consistent.

**Mode:** Carriage images + staff reference + service-object images + ambient audio  \
**Format:** 9:16, target 12 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = exact fictional carriage and berth; Image 4 = adult staff identity and uniform; Images 5–6 = tray and ticket geometry; Audio 1 = rail ambience only.

```text
Create a vertical service vignette aboard the fictional night train “NORTHLINE”. Preserve the corridor width, cabin numbering shapes without readable digits, berth layout, brass fixtures, and navy upholstery from Images 1–3. Preserve the staff member from Image 4 and the unbranded service objects from Images 5–6.

Begin in the corridor with a gentle stabilized walk behind the staff member carrying one tray. The door slides open left; cut inside without reversing direction. The staff member places the tray on the documented shelf, folds down the lower berth in one mechanically plausible motion, then draws the curtain as moonlit countryside moves consistently from right to left outside. End on the warm reading lamp and untouched tray.

Sound intent: steady rail rhythm, door slide, fabric, cup contact, no announcement or music. Keep corridor/cabin orientation, object count, staff identity, hand contact, window motion, and lighting stable. Avoid luxury claims, real rail branding, impossible floor plan, duplicated staff, changing tray contents, generated text, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Spatial continuity, rail-motion direction, service sequence, object persistence, and brand neutrality.

## MOB-004 Sidewalk Delivery Robot Handoff

**Use it for:** A friendly last-mile concept video with readable robot behavior and pedestrian priority.

**Mode:** Robot design images + route map + recipient image + optional motion reference  \
**Format:** 16:9, target 12 seconds  \
**Reference map:** Images 1–3 = exact original robot design; Image 4 = simplified route geometry; Image 5 = consenting adult recipient; Video 1 = wheel motion only.

```text
Create a concept demonstration of a small six-wheeled delivery robot on a private campus path. Preserve the original body, lid, lights, wheel count, sensor positions, and neutral finish from Images 1–3. Follow the route in Image 4: straight path, stop line, pedestrian crossing, then designated handoff bay.

Opening: low three-quarter follow as the robot travels at walking speed. A pedestrian approaches the crossing; the robot slows and stops fully behind the line, lights changing from soft cyan to amber. After the path clears, it proceeds to the bay. The adult recipient from Image 5 taps a physical button, the lid opens upward, and they remove one plain paper bag. End with lid closed and lights returning to cyan.

Sound intent: quiet motors, wheel texture, button tone, campus ambience, no speech. Keep pedestrian priority, wheel-ground contact, path geography, object count, light-state logic, and recipient identity exact. Avoid autonomous-safety claims, public-road operation, surveillance framing, logos, floating app UI, face scans, generated text, or watermark.
```

**Review:** Yield behavior, route continuity, wheel mechanics, state lights, privacy, and claim scope.
